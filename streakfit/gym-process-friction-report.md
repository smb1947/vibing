# Gym Process Friction Report

## Summary

From `6-stage1-findings-synthesis.md` and `structured-interview-theme-matrix.md`, the high-friction gym process is not just the workout. Students describe a full chain of steps that starts before entering the gym and continues after leaving it.

The core issue is that a gym session requires planning, preparation, transport, timing, workout execution, showering, food, and return logistics. When any one step becomes inconvenient, students may skip the gym even when they want to go.

## Pre-Gym Process

Students often need to:

- Decide whether the day is gym-feasible
- Find a usable time window around classes, work, lab, errands, or recruiting
- Eat early enough or prepare a snack/protein
- Pack clothes, shoes, towel, lock, water bottle, and shower items
- Carry a gym bag through the day
- Coordinate with a gym buddy or accountability partner
- Plan transport by walking, bus, driving, or parking
- Avoid crowded hours
- Mentally commit before other tasks take over

Common breakdowns include:

- Forgotten clothes
- Heavy bag
- Mistimed meals or hunger
- Infrequent bus or long walk
- Bad weather
- Crowded gym
- Low energy after class
- Schedule changes that erase the planned workout window

## During-Gym Process

Students do not only evaluate the workout. They evaluate whether the entire trip feels worth it.

Examples from the findings:

- Some students avoid crowded times because crowding makes the experience negative.
- Some prefer longer sessions because short workouts feel pointless after travel and shower time.
- Some choose apartment gyms or walking instead of the better campus gym because the convenient option has less overhead.
- Some rely on lockers, gym buddies, calendar invites, or pre-mapped workouts to reduce decision friction.

## Post-Gym Process

After the workout, students often still need to:

- Shower
- Change clothes
- Get food or protein
- Walk, bus, drive, or park again
- Return to campus, home, or another commitment
- Recover enough to continue the day

This post-gym overhead makes the total cost of a gym session much larger than the workout itself. For students with evening classes, lab work, job applications, errands, or home responsibilities, the return process can be enough to make them skip the gym.

## Core Problems To Solve

1. Students need to plan the whole gym trip, not just the workout.
2. Students need reminders for clothes, food, shower items, transport, and return logistics.
3. Students need a realistic total-time estimate that includes prep, travel, workout, shower, food, and return.
4. Students need low-friction fallback plans when the full gym trip is not feasible.
5. Students need ways to reduce bag friction, crowding risk, commute uncertainty, and restart difficulty.

## Website Prototype Direction

The website should work as a student gym-trip planner.

It should help a student answer:

- Is today a full gym day, short gym day, or fallback day?
- How much total time will the gym actually take?
- What do I need to pack before leaving?
- When should I eat?
- When should I go to avoid crowds?
- Do I need to shower after?
- What is the lowest-friction version of the workout if the full plan is not realistic?

## Suggested Website Features

### 1. Gym Feasibility Planner

Inputs:

- Available time window
- Energy level
- Commute type
- Shower needed or not
- Crowd tolerance
- Bag already packed or not
- Meal status

Output:

- Full gym plan
- Short gym plan
- Fallback workout
- Skip-risk warning

### 2. Total-Time Calculator

Calculate:

- Packing time
- Travel time to gym
- Workout time
- Waiting/crowding buffer
- Shower and change time
- Food/protein time
- Return travel time

This helps make the hidden overhead visible.

### 3. Packing And Prep Checklist

Checklist items:

- Gym clothes
- Shoes
- Towel
- Lock
- Water bottle
- Headphones
- Protein or snack
- Rain gear
- Shower items
- Clean clothes

The goal is to prevent one forgotten item from derailing the session.

### 4. Low-Friction Alternatives

If the full gym trip is not realistic, suggest:

- Apartment gym
- Walk
- Short bodyweight session
- Stretching
- Lower-intensity workout
- Weekend make-up session

This prevents an all-or-nothing failure.

### 5. Restart Support

For students who missed several sessions, the site should suggest a lower-pressure restart plan:

- 20-minute session
- Go during low-crowd hours
- Do a familiar workout
- Bring a buddy
- Treat the goal as restarting the habit, not maximizing performance

## One-Sentence Product Concept

A gym-trip planner that helps students reduce the hidden overhead of going to the gym by planning prep, commute, crowd timing, workout, shower, food, and return logistics in one place.
